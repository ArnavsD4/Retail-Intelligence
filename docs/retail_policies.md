# Retail Inventory Policies

## Policy 1: Reorder Planning

Retail stores should review inventory regularly using forecast demand, current stock, supplier lead time, and safety stock.

The reorder quantity should be calculated using a defined inventory target. A recommendation must not be generated from forecast demand alone without considering current stock.

## Policy 2: Stockout Risk

Stockout risk should be assessed by comparing available days of inventory against supplier lead time and the inventory review period.

- High risk: available inventory may not cover supplier lead time.
- Medium risk: inventory covers supplier lead time but may not cover the full protection period.
- Low risk: inventory covers the protection period.

These are demonstration rules for this project and should be configurable for different retailers.

## Policy 3: Demand Forecasting

Weekly demand forecasts should be evaluated against historical sales using time-based validation.

Forecasts should be compared using appropriate metrics such as MAE and RMSE. Business recommendations must use the numerical outputs of the forecasting system rather than estimates generated by a language model.

## Policy 4: Supplier Lead Time

Supplier lead time represents the expected number of days between placing an order and receiving inventory.

Longer lead times generally require more inventory protection. The system should use the lead time configured for the relevant product or supplier and should not invent missing values.
